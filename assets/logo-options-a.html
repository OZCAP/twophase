Option A, variations: Prepared → Committed

Same idea in each: a database whose top layer isn't committed yet. The differences are how “not yet” is drawn, and how well it survives at 16 px.

A1 · refined original

Floating slab

The dashed layer becomes a full slab with sides, hovering above the solid base. It reads as a layer not yet committed rather than a halo.

twophase
A2 · softer

Ghost layer

The prepared layer is translucent instead of dashed: a ghost of the change. Calmer, more premium, and much cleaner at small sizes than dashes.

twophase
A3 · bold

Two-tone stack

One cylinder in two bands: amber on top (waiting, the product's “ask” colour) over a white base (committed). The most legible at any size, and literally two phases.

twophase
A4 · motion

Slot-in

The top slab sits askew, about to drop into place. It implies the moment of commit, and it animates beautifully: on approve, the slab clicks into place.

twophase
A5 · explicit

Approved store

A solid database with a check cut through it: approved data. The most literal of the set. It says “database + approval” instantly, but loses the two-phase idea.

twophase
A6 · dev-tool

Line icon

A monoline outline with a dashed top section and solid base. It looks like a developer tool (think terminal or IDE icon sets) and pairs well with code in docs.
